# What the deferred first-order pass costs (proposal item 10)

Follows `PROPOSAL_2026-10-29.md` item 10 and `AUDIT_2026-10-27.md` ("the
deferral's cost ... unchanged and unmeasured").  No simulator source was
changed for this measurement; the model and the scripts are in this
directory.  Short version: **the lost draw the deferral was expected to cost
does not show up at any off rate**, so nothing about the deferral needs a
fix; what the measurement did turn up is three other things, two of them in
the serial reference, listed under "Verdict".

## The effect to measure

Under `nerdss_mpi` the x axis is cut into columns of `rMaxLimit`.  At the top
of a step every complex a rank owns with a member in its last owned column
(bin n-2) is held back from the first-order pass and drawn right after the
first exchange instead (`src/mpi/defer_edge_reactions.cpp`, 0363a361).  A
complex the right neighbour bound into during that half-step arrives with
`trajStatus == propagated` and `perform_held_back_first_order()` leaves it
out of the late pass, so the older bonds of such a complex take no
dissociation draw that step.  Each lost draw adds one step to a bond's
expected lifetime (the bond survives a step it should have risked), whatever
the off rate: the prediction is a longer mean lifetime for bonds whose complex
gained a member while they were alive, visible only at np > 1, growing with
the number of rank boundaries, proportional to the rate of binding into
existing complexes and -- in relative terms -- independent of the off rate.

## The model (`models/deferral`)

* **A**: a hub with four binding sites, tetrahedral at 1.5 nm, D = 10 nm²/µs,
  Dr = 0.1; **B**: one site at 0.75 nm, D = 20, Dr = 0.5.  B is monovalent,
  so no complex is larger than one A and four B and none spans a column.
  Every B that binds an A that already holds a B is "a member gained by a
  complex while its older bonds are alive".
* Four reactions `A(ai) + B(b) <-> A(ai!1).B(b!1)`, ka = 1e4 nm³/µs,
  σ = 2 nm, kb = 1e5 /s (varied below); timestep 0.5 µs, so 1/kb = 20 steps
  and the per-step break probability is p = 1 - exp(-kb dt) = 0.049 (0.181
  at kb = 4e5, 0.012 at 2.5e4).
* 300 A and 1000 B in 270 × 100 × 100 nm; `rMaxLimit` = 32.86 nm, so the box
  is 8 columns, which is what `nerdss_mpi` needs to run np=4 (1,3,3,1) and
  what np=2 splits 4,4.  So one column in eight (np=2) or three in eight
  (np=4) is an edge column the right neighbour binds into.
* At kb = 1e5 the run has ~820 bonds alive, bound fraction 0.67, mean bond
  lifetime 21.6 steps, and a live bond's complex gains a member 0.091 times
  per step (every 11 steps).  4000 steps give 116,000 complete bond lifetimes
  per run in the counting window (276,000 at kb = 4e5, 22,000 at 2.5e4).
* `assocDissocWrite = true`; every other periodic output off.  The code also
  writes `RESTARTS/restart<itr>.json` every 400 steps regardless; those
  snapshots (every molecule's position and bonds, gathered to one file at
  any np) are what localises the differences below.

## Commands

```bash
cd benchmarks/nerdss_optimized/mpi_validation
make -C ../../.. serial && make -C ../../.. mpi CFLAGS="-O3"
for kb in 1e5 4e5 2.5e4; do
  ./deferral_run.sh ../../../bin/nerdss serial $kb $OUT $(seq 301 316) &
  ./deferral_run.sh ../../../bin/nerdss_mpi 2 $kb $OUT $(seq 301 316) &
  ./deferral_run.sh ../../../bin/nerdss_mpi 4 $kb $OUT $(seq 301 316) &
  wait
done
./deferral_run.sh ../../../bin/nerdss_mpi 1 1e5 $OUT $(seq 301 316)   # np=1 control
./deferral_run.sh ../../../bin/nerdss_mpi 1 4e5 $OUT $(seq 301 304)
python3 deferral_table.py $OUT                # lifetimes, bound fraction, Welch t
python3 deferral_columns.py --bins 16 $OUT    # bound fraction by x bin
python3 deferral_hazard.py $OUT               # hazard by bond age, per rank, double fires
python3 deferral_drift.py $OUT/kb4e5 serial np1 np2 np4   # left-right imbalance
```

Seeds 301-316 are fresh (the audit used 1-21 and 101-140; `nerdss_mpi` is
reproducible per seed).  np=2 was not run at kb = 2.5e4 (the machine was
shared; np=4 has three boundaries to np=2's one and is the stronger test).
Bonds are counted if formed in steps [500, nItr - 20/(kb dt)], so none is
cut off by the end of the run; the bound fraction is the mean of the
copy-number rows after step 500 (merged `mergeOUT/copy_numbers_time.dat` at
np > 1) of bound A sites over 1200.  Standard errors are over the seeds
(per-run means); the Welch t is of the per-seed means against serial;
"pooled z" treats every bond as a sample and agrees with it.  Each run's
replay was checked: no bond made twice or broken before it was made, and
the bonds alive at the end equal the last copy-number row.  The machine was
shared with four other sessions (load 20-190 on 10 cores); wall times are
meaningless and not reported.

-0.0015 at np=1 (t = -3.0), -0.0033 at np=2 (t = -6.2), -0.0077 at np=4

offset it is additive per boundary: -0.0018 for the one boundary of np=2,
-0.0062 for the three of np=4, about -0.002 each.

### Is the deficit the deferral's?

The only thing the deferral does to binding is the serial rule: a molecule
the late pass dissociated keeps its candidate pairs with probability zero
for the rest of the step (`zero_partner_probvec()`), exactly what
`isDissociated` does to a top-of-step dissociation in the serial order.
A skipped dissociation draw cannot lower the bound fraction.  To test it
directly a copy of the tree was built in the session's scratchpad with
`hold_back_right_edge_first_order()` returning at once (the behaviour before
0363a361; nothing committed).  That binary runs at np=1 but **crashes at np=4
on every one of the 16 seeds** (bus error, after 4 s to 84 min), so on a
model this fast the pre-fix conflict -- this rank dissociates a dimer the
neighbour binds into through a ghost that predates the dissociation --
corrupts the state within the run, and the deferral is load-bearing here.
The deficit therefore cannot be attributed by switching the deferral off; by
the code it is not the deferral's, and the candidates are the pairs across a
rank boundary, which the right rank's first half draws through its ghost
copies (the audit's pair-count check covers only step 1 with identical
coordinates; its reweighting-history probe found 76.7% of edge pairs with
history against 77.8% interior after fix #2), and the ownership hand-over
of a complex the neighbour bound into, which then sits in the left rank's
column as the right rank's complex.

Where it comes from, as far as the logs say (`deferral_hazard.py`):

* **No hub ever breaks two bonds in one step, in any binary** (0 hub-steps
  with two BREAKs in 4 million breaks per configuration), nor forms two.
  Both binaries keep the rule of `check_dissociation()`: a molecule stops
  drawing after its first dissociation of the step, so a hub with k bonds
  breaks one with probability 1 - (1-p)^k and each bond's mean hazard is
  (1 - (1-p)^k)/k, whatever order the bonds are drawn in.
* **The per-bond hazard at fixed k.**  With that formula p = 0.1813 gives
  0.1813 / 0.1649 / 0.1500 / 0.1366 for k = 1..4.  Measured on two seeds
  each (bonds of age >= 1, k the hub's bond count at the top of the step):

  | | k = 1 | k = 2 | k = 3 | k = 4 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | expected | 0.1813 | 0.1649 | 0.1500 | 0.1366 |
  | serial | 0.1798 | 0.1633 | 0.1487 | 0.137 |
  | np1 | 0.1818 | 0.1653 | 0.1503 | 0.1372 |
  | np2 | 0.1809 | 0.1645 | 0.1505 | 0.1375 |

  The MPI binary draws what the rule says.  **Serial draws 0.8-1.1% too few
  dissociations at every occupancy** (k = 1: 3σ over the two seeds; k = 2
  and 3: 4-5σ).  The lifetime difference at kb = 4e5 is the serial binary's
  shortfall, not an excess under MPI.
* The two binaries draw dissociation on different code paths.  Serial
  (`EXEs/nerdss.cpp`) calls `check_for_unimolecular_reactions_population()`:
  per back reaction a binomial count of events over the bound pairs of that
  interface, random pairs picked, and a pick whose hub already dissociated
  this step is *dropped* (the count is decremented, not redrawn).  The MPI
  binary calls `check_for_unimolecular_reactions()`: per molecule, in list
  order, each bond in `bndlist` order (oldest first) until one fires.  The
  two agree on the per-hub rate in expectation; the 1% serial shortfall at
  k = 1, where no drop can happen, is not explained by this reading and is
  left as a follow-up.  The age pattern they produce differs and is
  expected: the per-molecule path draws a hub's oldest bond first, so under
  the MPI binary the hazard rises with bond age (np=2 at kb = 4e5: 0.161 at
  age 1, 0.165 at age 12), the population path has no age preference and
  serial's hazard falls with age (0.164 to 0.159) because old bonds sit on
  busier hubs.  That is the whole content of the "gainers shorter under MPI"
  cells in the tables: gainers are the old bonds.
* The relative shortfall scales roughly with p (0.2% at 1e5, 1% at 4e5,
  nothing resolved at 2.5e4); it is the serial dissociation draw that is off
  by a fraction that grows with k dt.

### The serial reference is not uniform along x at kb = 4e5

The restart snapshots of the *serial* runs at kb = 4e5 show a steady
left-right imbalance (`deferral_drift.py`; left half of the box minus right
half, mean over snapshots at itr >= 1200, ± SE over seeds):

| configuration | seeds | hubs left - right | free B left - right |
|---|---|---|---|
| serial | 16 | +18.6 ± 3.1 (t = +6.0; every seed positive) | -24.9 ± 3.6 (t = -6.8; 15 of 16 negative) |
| np1 | 4 | +23.1 ± 3.9 (t = +5.9) | +2.2 ± 7.2 |
| np2 | 16 | -1.5 ± 3.5 | -3.1 ± 3.4 |
| np4 | 11 | -4.9 ± 2.9 | +8.2 ± 3.4 (t = +2.4) |
| serial, B's site at -x in its frame | 4 | +9.6 ± 3.6 (t = +2.7) | -22.7 ± 2.9 (t = -7.9) |
| serial, B listed before A in the input | 4 | -1.9 ± 3.5 | +16.4 ± 8.1 (t = +2.0) |
| serial, kb = 1e5 | 16 | +4.4 ± 3.2 | -5.3 ± 2.0 (t = -2.6) |
| serial, kb = 2.5e4 | 16 | +5.8 ± 4.1 | -0.7 ± 1.0 |

Per column, serial's leftmost column fills from 33 hubs at itr 400 to 40 by
itr 2400 and stays there while the rightmost holds 29-34; free B runs 52-56
per column on the left and 65-72 on the right from itr 1200 on.  The box is
closed and the model has no x asymmetry: this is a drift of the hubs to -x
(and of the B they bind to +x) that the single-process step produces and
that grows with the reaction turnover (2.4 times more events per step at 4e5
than at 1e5, 12 times more than at 2.5e4).  np=1 of the MPI binary has it
too; np=2 and np=4 do not; mirroring B's site in its body frame does not
remove it; **listing B before A in the input removes the hub drift**.

The ingredient that depends on the listing order is partner choice.
`determine_if_reaction_occurs()` walks a molecule's candidate pairs in list
order and takes the first whose draw succeeds, so earlier entries are
favoured by a factor of order p per position; a failed molecule then zeroes
its pairs on the partners' lists, so each pair is decided once, by whichever
molecule the bimolecular loop reaches first -- in serial, by index, so by
the A hubs (indices 0-299) before any B.  The candidate list is filled by
`measure_separations_to_identify_possible_reactions()` walking the cells
x-fastest with a forward half-shell, so a molecule's partners in the cell to
its -x side are appended before those in its own cell and to +x.  A hub
therefore picks its -x candidates first, and `associate_box()` moves it
toward the chosen B by D_A/(D_A + D_B) of the gap.  Under np >= 2 every
molecule that crosses a boundary is appended to the receiving rank's list,
so after a few hundred steps A and B are interleaved and the two species'
preferences cancel.  Why the free-B imbalance does not flip with B first is
not settled on 4 seeds; the hub side is.

The consequence for this measurement: at kb = 4e5 the serial run is itself
biased by the order it processes molecules in, by amounts (a 20% density
gradient across the box, a 1% dissociation shortfall) larger than anything
the decomposition does, so serial-vs-MPI differences of a percent cannot be
read as MPI defects at that off rate, and the per-bin comparison there is
not reported.  At kb = 1e5 the drift is marginal and the boundary deficit
above stands.

## Verdict

1. **The deferral's lost draw: no bias beyond noise, at any off rate.**  The
   predicted +0.3-0.5% lengthening at kb = 1e5 is excluded at five standard
   errors; at 4e5 (prediction +0.6%, the lifetime is shorter instead) and at
   2.5e4 (-0.1 ± 0.2%) likewise.  The np=1 control shows that the small
   lifetime shifts that are there do not depend on the rank count.  **No
   change to the deferral is warranted**, and the alternative late pass the
   brief raised (drawing the older bonds of a neighbour-bound complex anyway,
   as a serial step would draw a dissociation before the association) has no
   measured defect to fix.  It would also be wrong in the other direction:
   the serial order forbids a molecule that associated this step from also
   dissociating in it, and that is what the exclusion reproduces.
2. **A binding deficit at rank boundaries is real** (kb = 1e5: about
   -0.002 of bound fraction per boundary on top of a -0.0015 np=1 offset,
   t = -6 at np=2 and -16 at np=4, present at 2.5e4 and 4e5 as well;
   locally a 9% shortfall within half a column left of each boundary, and
   no such bin at np=1).
   Not the deferral's by code, and untestable by removing the deferral,
   whose absence crashes this model at np=4.  Open; see follow-ups.
3. **The serial binary's dissociation draw is ~1% short at kb dt = 0.2** and
   **its partner choice drifts the hubs along -x** at high turnover; the MPI
   binary on one rank shares the drift, not the shortfall.  Neither is an
   MPI defect; both are findings about the reference.

## Follow-ups (not implemented)

* **Boundary binding deficit** (item 2).  Deterministic probe rather than
  statistics: on one np=2 step from a saved state, list every candidate pair
  with a member within `rMaxLimit` of the boundary on each rank and compare
  the pair set, the probabilities and the reweighting history with the
  serial step from the same coordinates; then the same after the first
  exchange for pairs the right rank's first half decided through ghosts.
  The audit's step-1 pair count is too early (identical coordinates, no
  history) to see this.
* **Serial dissociation shortfall** (item 3).  Instrument
  `check_for_unimolecular_reactions_population()` to count draws, drops and
  performed events per step at kb dt = 0.2 on this model; the k = 1
  shortfall means the binomial count or the pair list is short, not only
  the drop rule.
* **Order-dependent partner choice** (item 3).  Either test a molecule's
  candidates in a random order, or draw one partner with probability
  p_i / Σp and the event with probability min(1, Σp) -- the comment in
  `perform_bimolecular_reactions()` says the sequential test was chosen over
  summing, so this is a design decision for the maintainers.  Affects master
  as much as this branch; the MPI binary hides it only because it scrambles
  the index order.
* `deferral_hazard.py` and `deferral_drift.py` are generic enough to run on
  any assoc-dissoc log and restart set; a run of the drift check on a
  production model with high turnover would say whether the gradient matters
  outside this probe.

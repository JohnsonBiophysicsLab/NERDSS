# Wide-box scaling of nerdss_mpi against the serial build (2026-10-30)

Item 2 of `PROPOSAL_2026-10-29.md`: at which rank count does `nerdss_mpi`
beat `bin/nerdss` once the box is wide enough that an exchange ships a small
part of a rank's columns?  The audit's bench (`AUDIT_2026-10-27.md`) ran the
validation boxes, where hetTrimer has 8 columns of rMaxLimit and each
exchange ships two of a rank's four or five visible columns; there
`nerdss_mpi` was slower than serial at np=1, 2 and 4.  Nothing in the
simulator changed for this note; the builds are the tip of
`claude/mpi-audit-1-4` (07a384bc) plus the harness commits of this branch.

## Cases

`models/wide_box/`, listed in `scaling_cases.tsv`:

| case | box (nm) | rMaxLimit | columns | molecules | steps | from |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hetTrimer_wide | 1607.35 x 321.47 x 321.47 | 37.58 | 42 | 1665 A + 1665 B + 1665 C | 10000 | `VALIDATE_SUITE/hetTrimer`, 5x along x, copy numbers 5x |
| clathrin_wide | 2470 x 494 x 494 | 33.73 | 73 | 500 clat | 300000 | `VALIDATE_SUITE/clathrin` (kon 1 uM), 5x along x, copy number 5x |

Only the x length and the copy numbers differ from the validation inputs,
so concentrations, rates, time steps and the .mol files are the same.
`column_share()` splits 42 columns as 21+21 (np=2), 14+14+14 (np=3),
11+11+10+10 (np=4) and 6+6+5+5+5+5+5+5 (np=8); 73 as 37+36, 25+24+24,
19+18+18+18 and 10+9+9+9+9+9+9+9.
Every middle rank has at least the 3 columns the rule demands, so np=8 is
admitted on both.

## How it was run

```
make serial
make mpi CFLAGS="-O3"
V=benchmarks/nerdss_optimized/mpi_validation
REPS=2 $V/bench_mpi.sh <result-root> $V/scaling_cases.tsv \
    serial=bin/nerdss mpi_np1=bin/nerdss_mpi:1 mpi_np2=bin/nerdss_mpi:2 \
    mpi_np3=bin/nerdss_mpi:3 mpi_np4=bin/nerdss_mpi:4 mpi_np8=bin/nerdss_mpi:8
python3 $V/bench_table.py <result-root>/timing.tsv
```

Seed 20260810 (the bench's default) for every run, so the same trajectory
is simulated at every rank count up to the per-rank reseeding at np>1.  The
Mac (10 cores, 4 performance + 6 efficiency) was shared with four sessions
running MPI suites, at load 35-120 for most of the afternoon, so the batch
was driven one config at a time by a wrapper that waited for the 1-minute
load to stay below 3.5 for 90 s before each run (the equivalent of running
`bench_mpi.sh` once per config with `REP_START`=`REPS`=rep); every row of
`timing.tsv` carries the load before and after its run, and the tables take
the minimum over the passes.  `ranks.tsv` holds each rank's own totals.

What the two totals cover (`EXEs/nerdss_mpi.cpp`): the communication timer
brackets each of the two exchanges whole, from `claim_unowned_complexes()`
through packing, the sends, the receive's wait and unpacking, to
`perform_held_back_first_order()`; everything else in the step, including
`remove_empty_slots()` and the output, is computation.  So a rank's
communication total is pack + unpack + the time spent waiting for its
neighbours, and the wait includes any imbalance.

## Results

Runs of 2026-10-31, 01:07-02:22 (hetTrimer, then clathrin), load 0.9-3.9 at
the start of every run; np=3 added 2026-11-02 at load 1.5-3.8.  Two passes
per config, every pass within 1% of its twin (np=3: hetTrimer three passes,
129 s under a four-core software updater, then 122 and 120 s; clathrin two
passes within 2%); the tables show the minimum.
Raw rows: `results_scaling_2026-10-30/timing.tsv` and `ranks.tsv`.  Swap was
92% full during the np=3 runs (a nearly full swap has stalled single runs
on this Mac by 100x at low load, which is why every config was run more
than once); no run showed the stall signature of a pass far from its twin.

## Reading

**The exchange is the limit on wide boxes, not the physics.**  The
per-rank numbers say it directly:

* A rank's computation falls as 1/np, as it should: hetTrimer 2.8, 1.9, 1.5
  and 0.87 ms per step at np=2, 3, 4 and 8 (per owned column 4.0, 4.1, 4.1
  and 4.8 s;
  the np=8 excess is the Mac's six efficiency cores, which at least four of
  eight ranks must run on).  Nothing in the physics stops scaling.
* A rank's communication does not fall: 2.0, 1.8 and 1.8 ms per step at
  np=3, 4 and 8 on hetTrimer, 77, 80 and 105 us on clathrin.  Each exchange ships the
  shared column, the ghost column and the complexes touching them whatever
  np is, and the receive waits for the neighbour's whole half-step, so the
  cost is a constant per boundary.  On a 42-column box it is already 45% of
  a rank's time at np=2 and two thirds at np=8.
* So the step time has a floor at the exchange cost: on hetTrimer about
  1.8 ms of the serial build's 4.25 ms, which caps `nerdss_mpi` at about
  2.3x the serial build on this box however many ranks are added (measured:
  1.56x at np=8, where computation still adds 0.87 ms).  On clathrin the
  floor, 80-105 us per step, equals the serial step itself (105 us): at
  np=8 a rank spends on the two exchanges what the serial build spends on
  the whole step, before doing any physics.  At that density `nerdss_mpi`
  cannot beat serial at any rank count, and np=8 is no faster than np=4
  because the exchange grew from 80 to 105 us per step as the rendezvous
  chain got longer (proposal item 5) while computation fell by only 21 us.

What the exchange cost is made of is not split by the timers (item 5 asks
for that first).  The np=2 profile in `PROPOSAL_2026-10-29.md` puts
`MPI_Wait` at 36-45% of a rank, pack + unpack at 25-37% and the walk at
17-21%, i.e. about half of the communication total is waiting on the
neighbour (its half-step plus the chain) and half is serialisation,
re-indexing and the copies they make.

**The np=1 penalty is the other half of the np=2 loss.**  `nerdss_mpi` at
np=1 costs 1.31x the serial step on hetTrimer (5.57 against 4.25 ms) and
2.0x on clathrin (207 against 105 us, where the per-step fixed costs,
`remove_empty_slots()` and the third cell-list rebuild, are a larger share
of a cheap step).  That is item 1 of the proposal (the pre-#73 walk and
the per-step bookkeeping).  With it, np=2 would need the exchange alone to
be under half a serial step to win; today the exchange is 2.4 ms against
4.25.

**What this says for items 3-6.**  On a 42-column box the crossover is
already at np=3-4 with nothing changed, so the exchange work is about
moving the crossover down and raising the ceiling, not about making MPI
viable: if the communication total went to zero, np=4 would be 2.9x and
np=8 5x the serial build on hetTrimer.  Items 3-4 (the id maps and the
cheaper pack/unpack) attack the serialisation half; item 5 the waiting
half; item 6 (send only what changed) the volume.  Items 1 and 3-5 are the
ones that would let a box like clathrin_wide win at all, since there the
fixed cost per exchange, not the volume, is what equals the serial step.

**Limits of the measurement.**  One Mac with 4 performance and 6
efficiency cores: np=8 is a mixed-core result and understates what eight
equal cores would do, but it does not touch the np=4 conclusion.  One
seed; the two passes per config agree to 1%, and the trajectories differ
between rank counts only through the per-rank reseeding, so the physics
work per step is the same within noise (final copy numbers in
`timing.tsv`).  Start-up (placing 4995 molecules) is about 2 s of every
hetTrimer run and is inside the wall time.

# Proposed changes after the MPI audit (2026-10-29)

Follows `AUDIT_2026-10-27.md` (draft PR #94, branch `claude/mpi-audit-1-4`).
The audit left `nerdss_mpi` correct in every suite it ran, and slower than
the serial build at every rank count up to 4.  This note ranks what to do
about that and about the items the audit left open.  Nothing here is
implemented.

## Two new measurements behind the ranking

Both are `sample` profiles (1 ms, 8 s) of hetTrimer (`sample_inputs`, 321 nm
box, rMaxLimit 37.6 nm, so 8 columns in x) on the final `-O3` binaries.

**np=1 against the serial build.**  No communication happens at np=1, yet
the bench put `nerdss_mpi` at 1.34x (homoTrimer) to 2.98x (mem_localization)
the serial time.  Inclusive shares of a rank's samples:

| function | nerdss_mpi np=1 | serial |
|---|---|---|
| pair walk (`measure_separations_to_identify_possible_reactions` / the walk inlined in `nerdss.cpp`) | 66.8% | 55% |
| of which `check_bimolecular_reactions` | 49.4% | 46.8% |
| `check_overlap` + propagation | 9.9% | 9.3% |
| `perform_bimolecular_reactions` | 8.8% | 1.6% |
| `SimulVolume::update_memberMolLists` (3 calls per step vs 2) | 2.9% | 0.9% |
| `remove_empty_slots` (every step; the serial loop never calls it) | 2.5% | 0 |
| MPI bookkeeping (`derive_ghost_from_ownership`, `hold_outside_region`, `claim_unowned_complexes`, `drop_crossings_decided_elsewhere`) | 1.3% | 0 |

The np=1 run did about half the steps of the serial run in the same wall
time, so the equal shares mean the MPI walk costs about twice as much per
step.  The reason is in the code, not the profile: `nerdss.cpp`'s walk has
the type mask of PR #73 (`interactionMask`, `SubBox::typeMask`), the shell
index and the compartment gate; `measure_separations_to_identify_possible_
reactions.cpp` is the pre-#73 walk and offers every pair.  mem_localization,
where 99.6% of the offered pairs are lipid-lipid, is exactly the model with
the worst np=1 ratio.

**np=2, per rank** (from the audit's profile, re-read by function):

| | rank 0 | rank 1 |
|---|---|---|
| `MPI_Wait` on the receive | 35.7% | 44.9% |
| pack (`serialize_molecules` + `serialize_complexes`) | 22.0% | 20.7% |
| unpack (`deserialize_*`, after the wait) | 15.0% | 5.7% |
| of which `find_molecule` / `find_complex` | 7.3% | 6.8% |
| pair walk | 21.2% | 17.4% |
| `operator new` + `free` (copies made while packing and unpacking) | 11.0% | 10.2% |

Each exchange ships the shared column, the ghost column and every complex
touching them, whole: on an 8-column box at np=2 that is at least two of a
rank's four or five visible columns per half-step, so pack + unpack + wait
is as large as the physics by construction.  No change to the exchange's
code can make np=2 beat serial on boxes this narrow; the exchange cost is a
constant per boundary while the physics scales with the columns per rank.

## Performance, in the order to do them

1. **One pair walk for both builds.**  Move the serial walk (the type mask,
   `SubBox::typeMask`, the shell index, the compartment gate) into
   `measure_separations_to_identify_possible_reactions()` and have
   `nerdss.cpp` call it too, with the ghost rules under `#ifdef mpi_` as
   they are now.  Expected: np=1 within ~10% of serial on every model (the
   remaining 2-4% is `remove_empty_slots` and the third cell-list rebuild,
   which are worth a look in the same change).  Exact: the mask skips only
   pairs the function rejects, and nerdss_mpi is reproducible per seed, so
   every `-O3` np=1/2/3/4 output must be byte-identical before and after.
   Serial outputs must stay byte-identical too (`run_suite.sh`).  This
   comes first because np=1 parity is what makes `nerdss_mpi` usable at all
   and it is the only item that pays on every box size.

2. **A scaling benchmark before touching the exchange.**  Add a case to
   `bench_mpi.sh` with a wide box (hetTrimer or clathrin at 40+ columns,
   several thousand molecules) and run np=1, 2, 4, 8 against serial.  The
   result decides whether items 3-5 matter: if nerdss_mpi beats serial on
   wide boxes already after item 1, the exchange work is about scaling the
   crossover down, not about making MPI viable.

3. **Id-to-index maps.**  `find_molecule()` / `find_complex()` are linear
   scans per received molecule and per bound partner (7% of a rank at np=2
   and quadratic in the stripe population).  Ids are dense ints handed out by
   `Molecule::numberOfMolecules` / `Complex::numberOfComplexes`, so a
   `vector<int>` indexed by id, kept by the places that install, move and
   empty slots (`deserialize_*`, `destroy`), replaces both scans; validate
   each hit by `id ==` and `!isEmpty`, which is what the scans test today.
   `remove_empty_slots()` renumbers indices every MPI step, so the map is
   rebuilt there (one O(N) pass, cheaper than one scan).  Exact; outputs
   byte-identical.

4. **Cheaper pack and unpack**, three independent exact changes:
   * write ids to the wire directly instead of `indices_to_IDs()` rewriting
     the molecule in place and the caller saving and restoring
     `prevReweight` and `myComIndex` (one vector copy per molecule sent);
   * unpack into the existing slot instead of a temporary `Molecule` that is
     then copy-assigned (one more copy per molecule received);
   * replace `set<int>` with a marked `vector<int>`, and drop
     `serialize_complexes()`' reset of `deleteIfNotReceivedBack` over every
     complex (an O(N) pass per exchange).
   `operator new`/`free` are 10-11% of a rank today; most of it is these.

5. **Overlap the exchange with the half-step**: post the `MPI_Irecv` before
   the half-step's physics and do not `MPI_Wait` on the `MPI_Isend` until
   the buffer is next reused (`increase_send_buffers()` already owns the
   buffers).  Removes the rendezvous chain at np>=3 (rank r's send cannot
   complete until r-1 has posted its receive, which it does only after its
   own send completed).  Modest at np=2, where the wait is mostly the
   neighbour's compute.  Before this, split the existing computation /
   communication totals into wait and pack/unpack so the gain can be seen.

6. **Send only what changed** (the large one; only after 2 says it pays).
   A molecule the half-step neither moved nor rebonded is already current
   on the receiver; send its id alone, and replace the
   `deleteIfNotReceivedBack` rule with an explicit list of ids still present.
   Cuts pack and unpack by the unchanged fraction (most of the stripe on
   slow-diffusing or membrane models).  The conflict rules
   (`changed_since()`, `keptLocalCom`) are unaffected: they already decide
   by change, not by presence.

## Open items from the audit, with a proposal for each

7. **Complexes wider than two columns** (restart refused; a new run forms
   them silently).  The decomposition needs a complex inside a rank's
   visible columns (owned plus one ghost on each side).  Proposal: a cheap
   per-step check under `CHECK_STRUCTURE` and a one-line warning in the
   `-O3` build when any complex's x-extent exceeds the rank's visible width,
   so a Gag or clathrin lattice that outgrows the stripe is reported rather
   than wrong.  Widening the ghost region to k columns is the fix if it
   ever triggers; it is a parameter of `init_x_domain_and_offset()` and the
   exchange loops, not a redesign.

8. **RNG state at restart is rank 0's.**  Write `rng_state_<rank>` for
   every rank in the gathered checkpoint, read them back when the rank
   count matches, fall back to seed + rank otherwise (what happens today).
   Small; makes an np=N restart continue the np=N stream.

9. **Output merging.**  `merge_outputs()` covers copy numbers, the complex
   histogram and PDB/CIF frames; `bound_pair`, `mono_dimer`,
   `event_counters`, `observables` and the transition matrix are per-rank
   partial tallies that sum, and `assoc_dissoc_time` concatenates and sorts
   by time.  Mechanical; `checkpoint_check.sh` already knows how to compare
   the merged files to a serial run.

10. **What the deferral costs.**  A complex the neighbour bound into takes no
    first-order draw that step.  Measure it first with a high-koff model
    whose binding is concentrated at the boundary (`assoc_dissoc_check.sh`
    gives the per-bond lifetime); if the lifetime is biased beyond noise,
    let the late pass draw for the older bonds of such a complex, which
    the serial order allows (first-order before bimolecular in one step).

11. **Known leftovers** (two owners between a pass and its exchange,
    `ownerRank` on recycled slots): leave as they are, guarded by the
    conflict report and ownership checker; have `Complex::destroy()` reset
    `ownerRank` to -1 as a one-liner, since the recycled-slot case is only
    unreachable by the current ordering.

12. **Refusals by design** (compartments; the 3-column rule for middle
    ranks).  Keep.  Item 2's benchmark will show whether anyone should run
    np>=3 on an 8-column box at all.

13. **The standalone MPI branch** (upstream-dev/mpi f1dd0590): wrong at
    np=4 and slower than serial everywhere; retire it in favour of
    `nerdss_mpi` rather than port anything from it.

## Verification for every item

Serial suites byte-identical to the 1.4 tip (`run_suite.sh` +
`compare_suites.sh`); nerdss_mpi outputs byte-identical per seed at
np=1/2/3/4 for the exact items (1, 3, 4, 5 and 11), the checker, ASan and
`-O3` suites (`run_mpi_suite.sh`) for the rest; `bench_mpi.sh` before and
after, on a quiet machine, with the wide-box case of item 2 added; GCC 15
clean-tree `make serial`, `make checks`, `make mpi`.
